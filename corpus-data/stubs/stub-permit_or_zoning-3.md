## Background

Synthesis Development Group LLC filed Comprehensive Plan Amendment Application
CPA-2024-003 on January 31, 2024, requesting a large-scale future land use
map amendment for two contiguous parcels totaling 47.3 acres. The current
Agriculture (AG-1) designation limits residential density to 1 unit per 5
acres and prohibits commercial uses other than farm-direct retail stands.
The applicant proposes redesignation to Mixed Use Employment Center (MUEC),
which permits a range of light industrial, office, and residential uses at up
to 15 units per acre in the residential component.

The Florida Department of Commerce (formerly Florida Department of Economic
Opportunity) must review large-scale comprehensive plan amendments under Fla.
Stat. §163.3184. This matter was submitted to the Department on March 8, 2024,
and the 60-day objection window closes May 7, 2024. As of the date of this
memorandum, no objection letter has been received, and staff anticipates
adoption of the amendment at the June 2024 Board meeting.

This opinion addresses three legal risk areas: constitutional challenge exposure,
Agricultural Land classification implications, and developer's agreement
requirements.

## Legal Analysis

### Constitutional Takings Exposure

Approving a large-scale comprehensive plan amendment upzoning privately owned
land from AG-1 to MUEC does not, by itself, create inverse condemnation
liability. The applicant receives an increase in development entitlement;
no diminution in value of the applicant's property occurs. The constitutional
concern runs in the opposite direction: neighboring agricultural landowners
could argue that the MUEC upzoning places incompatible uses adjacent to
their agricultural operations, diminishing their property values or creating
nuisance conditions under Fla. Stat. §823.14 (Florida Right to Farm Act).

The Right to Farm Act provides immunity from nuisance suits for agricultural
operations that predate adjacent development if the agricultural operation
was established before the zoning change that brought the incompatible use.
The parcels to the north and east of the subject site are actively farmed
citrus and row-crop operations that predate any residential or commercial
development in this corridor. The Right to Farm Act immunity runs in their
favor, not in favor of any future MUEC development.

Counsel assesses this as a moderate legal risk. A developer who encounters
pre-existing agricultural operations after the MUEC development is built
will be unable to successfully claim nuisance against the farming neighbors.
This should be disclosed to the applicant in the developer's agreement and
in any approval resolution to create a clear record that the County did not
represent the site as free of agricultural compatibility issues.

### Agricultural Land Classification — Ad Valorem Implications

The subject parcels currently carry an agricultural land classification under
Fla. Stat. §193.461 (Florida Greenbelt Law), which substantially reduces
their assessed value for ad valorem tax purposes. Approval of the CPA does
not automatically remove the agricultural classification. Reclassification
requires a Property Appraiser determination, which typically occurs at the
next annual assessment cycle following a change in use.

If Synthesis Development Group LLC proceeds to site plan approval and
construction under the new MUEC designation without allowing the agricultural
classification to lapse, there is a risk of back-assessment under §193.461(3)(f)
for up to 5 years. Counsel recommends that the adoption resolution reference
this risk and require the applicant to certify at each site plan submission
whether the agricultural classification is still active.

### Developer's Agreement Requirements

Under Fla. Stat. §163.3220 and Manatee County LDC §8.7, the applicant has
proposed to execute a Developer's Agreement that would vest MUEC entitlements
for 15 years in exchange for commitments to provide internal roadway
improvements and a proportionate-share contribution to the widening of the
adjacent State Road. The proposed agreement was submitted on April 22, 2024,
as Exhibit F of MC-620148.

Three legal issues in the proposed Developer's Agreement require resolution
before the Board's adoption vote:

1. The proportionate-share calculation uses a traffic analysis dated October 2022
   that predates the corridor's current congestion conditions. An updated
   analysis should be required before the agreement is executed, or the
   agreement should include a recalculation trigger if the FDOT level-of-service
   standard is amended within the 15-year vesting period.

2. The agreement's reverter clause (§9.4 of the draft) purports to rescind
   all MUEC entitlements if construction has not commenced within 5 years.
   Under Florida law, a blanket reverter to the prior land use designation
   is a quasi-legislative act that requires another public hearing and cannot
   be effected by administrative determination alone. The agreement should
   be revised to describe the reverter as a staff recommendation to the
   Board to initiate a CPA back to AG-1, not an automatic reverter.

3. The indemnification clause (§12.1) indemnifies the County against third-party
   claims arising from the Developer's Agreement but does not specify whether
   the indemnification survives transfer of ownership of the parcels. This
   should be clarified to run with the land.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel recommends the Board approve CPA-2024-003
on its merits but condition adoption on: (a) execution of an updated traffic
analysis or a recalculation-trigger provision, (b) revision of the Developer's
Agreement reverter clause to describe a recommendation rather than an automatic
reverter, and (c) clarification that the indemnification obligation runs with
the land. With these three modifications, the legal risk profile of the CPA
is acceptable. The constitutional takings risk is low. The Right to Farm
Act compatibility issue should be disclosed to the applicant before adoption.

Counsel will work with Growth Management staff and the applicant's counsel
