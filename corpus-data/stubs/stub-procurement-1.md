## Background

Manatee County Purchasing issued Invitation to Bid ITB-2024-0037 on February 1,
2024, seeking a three-year contract for preventive maintenance, emergency repair,
and load-bank testing services for the County's fleet of 47 emergency standby
generators at 23 facilities county-wide. The estimated contract value is
$1.2 million over the three-year base term with two one-year renewal options.

Bids were received on March 8, 2024. Three responsive bids were submitted:

| Bidder | Base Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Three-Year Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidated Power Systems Corp. | $381,400 | $390,100 | $399,200 | $1,170,700 |
| TurboGen Maintenance Solutions Inc. | $374,200 | $388,500 | $403,000 | $1,165,700 |
| Southern Standby Service LLC | $421,800 | $432,500 | $443,600 | $1,297,900 |

Staff recommended award to Consolidated Power Systems Corp. on March 15,
2024. TurboGen filed a protest on March 22, 2024, asserting: (1) that
Consolidated's bid was non-responsive because it did not include a completed
Schedule of Unit Rates Addendum (Addendum 3); and (2) that the Purchasing
Director did not provide adequate bid tabulation access before posting the
staff recommendation.

## Legal Analysis

### Ground 1 — Non-Responsiveness of Consolidated's Bid

Addendum 3 was issued on February 23, 2024, and required bidders to complete
a Schedule of Unit Rates for 14 individual generator service tasks. The
addendum notice stated: "Completion of the Schedule of Unit Rates is required
for bid responsiveness."

Examination of Consolidated's bid package (MC-700337 through MC-700344)
confirms that Consolidated submitted its bid without the completed Schedule
of Unit Rates. Consolidated instead attached a cover letter stating that its
lump-sum annual fees include all unit-rate services and that a separate
schedule was not applicable to its pricing model.

Under Manatee County Procedure 26-112.003 §4(c), a bid is non-responsive
if it omits a required form identified in the solicitation as a condition of
responsiveness. The Addendum 3 language is explicit. The Purchasing Director's
staff recommendation memorandum (MC-700355) acknowledges the missing form but
characterizes the omission as a minor irregularity under §4(d) because the
lump-sum pricing still permitted apples-to-apples evaluation.

Counsel's analysis: The distinction between a "required form" and a "minor
irregularity" is a close call on these facts. The waiver-of-minor-irregularity
doctrine under Florida procurement law permits waiver only if the irregularity
does not give the bidder a competitive advantage and does not affect the
County's ability to evaluate the bid on equal terms with other bids. Here,
the Schedule of Unit Rates was intended to allow the County to assess the
unit cost of individual services outside the annual retainer — e.g., for
surge-event work beyond the preventive maintenance scope. By omitting the
schedule, Consolidated did not price those tasks individually, while TurboGen
and Southern Standby did. If the contract were to generate substantial unit-rate
work above the retainer, the County would be without a negotiated rate with
Consolidated. This creates a material evaluation disparity, not a minor
irregularity.

Counsel assesses that the protest Ground 1 is legally meritorious. The
Purchasing Director's decision to waive the omission is defensible only if
the unit-rate schedule had no material evaluation impact — and on this record,
that determination is difficult to defend. Recommendation: rebid or cure, as
discussed in Section 5 below.

### Ground 2 — Tabulation Access

TurboGen asserts that the bid tabulation was not made available for public
inspection before the staff recommendation was posted, as required by
Procedure 26-112.003 §6(a). Purchasing file notes (MC-700372 through MC-700381)
confirm that the bid tabulation worksheet was posted to the County's Vendor
Self-Service portal on March 15, 2024 — the same day as the staff recommendation.
Procedure 26-112.003 §6(a) requires posting "prior to issuance of the staff
recommendation."

On these facts, the procedural sequence was inverted. The Purchasing Director
disputes that simultaneous posting violates §6(a), arguing that "prior to"
means "in advance of the Board's award vote" rather than "in advance of the
staff recommendation." Counsel's reading of the plain text of §6(a) is that
it means prior to the staff recommendation, not prior to the Board vote.
However, TurboGen suffered no prejudice from the simultaneous posting because
it received the tabulation on the same day as the recommendation, leaving 21
calendar days before the protest deadline — well within the protest period.

Ground 2 is a procedural irregularity but not a prejudicial one. Courts and
administrative tribunals in Florida have generally declined to void procurement
awards based on procedural violations that caused no prejudice to the
protesting bidder. Ground 2 alone would not support voiding the award.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — The meritorious protest ground is Ground 1.
Counsel recommends that the Purchasing Director sustain the protest in part
on Ground 1 and reject it in part on Ground 2. The available remedies are:

1. **Rebid ITB-2024-0037.** Clean resolution. Estimated delay of 45-60 days.
   All three bidders remain eligible. Preferred if total contract value
   justifies the rebid cost.

2. **Issue a cure notice to Consolidated.** Under Procedure 26-112.003 §4(e),
   the Purchasing Director may allow a responsive bidder to cure an omission
   that is not material to price. If the County determines that unit-rate
   work is unlikely to exceed 10% of the total contract value, a cure notice
   requiring Consolidated to submit a Schedule of Unit Rates within 5 business
   days — priced consistently with its lump-sum bid — might be defensible.
   However, a cure notice here carries risk if TurboGen escalates to circuit
   court, because the cure effectively re-opens pricing for one bidder.

3. **Award to TurboGen as lowest responsive bidder.** TurboGen's three-year
   total of $1,165,700 is $5,000 below Consolidated's. The price difference
   is immaterial at 0.43% of contract value. If the County determines Consolidated
   is non-responsive, awarding to TurboGen avoids a rebid. This option
   eliminates the Ground 1 dispute entirely.

Counsel recommends Option 1 (rebid) if the Purchasing Director believes
unit-rate work will represent more than 10% of contract value; otherwise
Option 3 (award to TurboGen) if speed of award is the priority. Option 2
carries undue litigation risk.

Please advise which path the Purchasing Director prefers before the Board
award agenda is finalized. Contact Jerome Wickfield at MC-700381 Ext. 3142.
