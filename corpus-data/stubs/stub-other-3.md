## Background

Tropical Storm Synthetic (fictional) made landfall in Manatee County on
August 24, 2023, producing sustained winds of 63 mph and 12.4 inches of
rainfall over a 36-hour period. The storm caused widespread tree fall, localized
flooding in low-lying areas of the unincorporated county, and approximately
4,200 power outages. The Board of County Commissioners adopted Resolution
R-2023-E-012 on August 25, 2023, declaring a county-wide state of emergency
for 30 days.

Emergency Management Director Lindquist has requested a consolidated legal
advisory on four areas of authority during the emergency period:

1. Emergency procurement authority — waiver of competitive bidding
2. Temporary suspension of permit requirements for emergency repairs
3. Authority to commandeer private property for debris staging
4. Curfew and emergency order authority

This memorandum addresses all four.

## Legal Analysis

### Issue 1 — Emergency Procurement Authority

Under Ch. 2-26 Procedure 26-112.009, the County Administrator may waive
competitive bidding requirements during a declared emergency for procurements
reasonably necessary to protect public health, safety, and welfare. The waiver
authority is limited to: (a) the duration of the state of emergency declaration;
(b) procurements directly related to emergency response and recovery; and (c)
amounts not to exceed $500,000 per procurement action without Board ratification.

The Board may ratify emergency procurements in excess of $500,000 at its next
regular or emergency meeting following the procurement. Ratification should
be sought at the September 12, 2023 regular Board meeting for any emergency
contracts executed before that date.

Key documentation requirements for FEMA Public Assistance reimbursement:
Emergency procurement records must include (a) a written determination by the
County Administrator that competitive bidding was impracticable given the
emergency; (b) a price reasonableness analysis (verbal confirmation from at
least 2 vendors of market rate, documented in writing immediately after the
call); (c) a scope-of-work description sufficient for FEMA eligibility review.
FEMA PA guidance (MC-890658 through MC-890668) requires this documentation
for Category A (debris removal) and Category B (emergency protective measures)
costs. Failure to document price reasonableness is the single most common
basis for FEMA PA cost disallowance.

Counsel recommends Emergency Management designate two staff members as
procurement documentation liaisons responsible solely for creating contemporaneous
price-reasonableness records during the emergency period.

### Issue 2 — Temporary Permit Relief

Manatee County LDC §6.2(f) authorizes the Building Official to issue a blanket
emergency repair authorization during a declared state of emergency, waiving
standard permit and inspection requirements for repairs to residential
structures that are (a) necessary to prevent further damage or restore habitability
and (b) valued at $10,000 or less per structure. Repairs above $10,000 require
at minimum an emergency permit issued within 24 hours of application.

Structural repairs to primary load-bearing elements (roof trusses, bearing
walls, foundation) are not eligible for the blanket emergency authorization
regardless of value — they require an emergency permit with engineering review.
This limitation is important to communicate to property owners and contractors
in the public advisory messaging.

The Building Official should issue a written Emergency Repair Authorization
(ERA) immediately, posting it to the County website and County Clerk's office,
that: (a) identifies the effective date (retroactive to August 25, 2023);
(b) lists the categories of work covered and excluded; and (c) states the
blanket authorization expires at the end of the emergency declaration period
(30 days from R-2023-E-012, i.e., September 24, 2023).

### Issue 3 — Commandeering Private Property for Debris Staging

Manatee County Emergency Ordinance §2-26-108.001(d) and Fla. Stat. §252.36(5)
authorize the County to commandeer private property for emergency use during
a declared state of emergency. The commandeering authority includes use of
private land for debris staging areas, provided:

1. The use is reasonably necessary for emergency operations.
2. The owner is notified in writing at the time of commandeering (or as soon
   as practicable if immediate oral authorization was required).
3. The County pays just compensation for the use, calculated at fair market
   rental value for comparable property for the duration of use.

Counsel recommends Emergency Management identify two or three candidate
staging sites in the northwest and southeast quadrants of the unincorporated
county. Priority should be given to sites where the County can negotiate a
voluntary temporary use agreement on an expedited basis, avoiding the complexity
of commandeering. Voluntary agreements should include: (a) specific duration
(not to exceed 90 days); (b) restoration obligations (County returns property
to its pre-use condition within 30 days of agreement termination); (c) daily
rental rate at fair market value; and (d) indemnification of the property owner
for third-party claims arising from the County's use.

If commandeering is exercised involuntarily, the County must initiate
compensation proceedings within 60 days under Fla. Stat. §252.36(5)(c).

### Issue 4 — Curfew and Emergency Order Authority

Resolution R-2023-E-012 delegates emergency order authority to the County
Administrator for the duration of the declaration. Under Fla. Stat. §252.38(3)(b),
a county may impose curfews, control ingress and egress, and restrict movement
within an emergency area during a declared state of emergency.

If the County Administrator determines that a curfew is necessary in a defined
geographic area (e.g., severely damaged neighborhoods in Palmetto and Ellenton),
the curfew order must: (a) be in writing; (b) identify the geographic area
by street boundaries or parcel range, not general descriptors; (c) state the
hours and duration; (d) identify the legal authority (Fla. Stat. §252.38(3)(b)
and Resolution R-2023-E-012); and (e) provide for emergency vehicle, utility
crew, and essential worker exemptions.

Curfew violations are a second-degree misdemeanor under §252.38(3)(b). Law
enforcement should be briefed on the written order terms before any enforcement
action. Consistency of enforcement is critical — selective enforcement creates
equal protection exposure.
