## Background

Facilities Management Division submitted Sole-Source Justification Request
SS-2024-0019 on July 31, 2024, seeking County Administrator authorization
to procure a direct digital controls (DDC) system upgrade for the Civic Center
Complex from Integrated Building Controls Corp. without competitive solicitation.
The Civic Center Complex comprises four interconnected buildings (Buildings A,
B, C, and D) totaling 148,000 square feet of conditioned space.

The existing DDC system was installed by IBC Corp. in 2009 under a competitively
bid contract and was last upgraded in 2017. The justification memorandum
submitted by Facilities (MC-801562 through MC-801571) asserts three grounds
for sole-source procurement under Procedure 26-112.007:

1. **Proprietary compatibility**: The existing 2009 IBC Corp. DDC system
   uses a proprietary communications protocol (IBC-Modbus variant, designated
   Version 2.3) that is not natively readable by third-party DDC systems.
   Upgrading to a non-IBC system would require replacing all 218 existing
   field controllers at an estimated additional cost of $920,000.

2. **Emergency operational necessity**: The existing DDC system's central
   server reached end-of-vendor-support in January 2024. The server currently
   runs on an unsupported Windows Server 2012 platform. Facilities reports
   two server instability events in June 2024 and one in August 2024 that
   caused loss of HVAC control for up to 4 hours per event.

3. **No alternative source**: Facilities asserts that no other vendor can
   upgrade the existing IBC Corp. field controllers to current standards
   without a full rip-and-replace at the $920,000+ cost.

## Legal Analysis

### Sole-Source Standard under Procedure 26-112.007

Procedure 26-112.007 §3 identifies four authorized bases for sole-source
procurement: (a) proprietary compatibility when alternatives would require
replacement of existing infrastructure; (b) emergency necessity affecting
public health, safety, or essential government operations; (c) no alternative
source available in the market; and (d) County Administrator waiver for
procurements below $50,000. This request invokes bases (a), (b), and (c).

### Ground (a) — Proprietary Compatibility

The IBC Corp. proprietary protocol documentation (MC-801597 through MC-801605)
confirms that the IBC-Modbus v2.3 protocol requires IBC Corp. hardware for
field controller integration. An independent HVAC controls consultant engaged
by Facilities (MC-801606 through MC-801618) reviewed the existing system and
confirmed that three competitor DDC platforms — Siemens Desigo, Honeywell
Building Manager, and Johnson Controls Metasys — cannot natively integrate
with IBC-Modbus v2.3 without gateway converters. The consultant estimates
gateway converters would add $140,000 to the project cost and reduce reliability
by introducing conversion latency. This analysis supports ground (a).

However, the consultant's report raises a qualification: Automated Logic
Corporation, a DDC vendor not contacted by Facilities, offers a third-party
integration module for IBC-Modbus v2.3 at an estimated cost of $85,000 for
the 218-controller installation. The report notes this option was not evaluated
because Facilities assumed IBC Corp. was the only vendor with native compatibility.
This is a significant gap in the sole-source documentation. The County Administrator
should require Facilities to obtain a written price and scope from Automated
Logic Corporation before authorizing sole-source procurement. If the Automated
Logic option is technically viable and the total-cost difference is material,
the procurement should be competitive.

### Ground (b) — Emergency Necessity

The server instability events documented by Facilities (MC-801570 through
MC-801573) represent genuine operational risk. Loss of HVAC control at a
public building complex serving an estimated 1,200 daily visitors is an
operational disruption that qualifies as "affecting essential government
operations" under Procedure 26-112.007 §3(b). The end-of-vendor-support
status of Windows Server 2012 also presents a cybersecurity vulnerability
consistent with the County's IT Security Policy.

Counsel agrees that urgency supports an expedited procurement. However,
urgency does not automatically eliminate the requirement for competitive
solicitation — Procedure 26-112.007 §3(b) authorizes sole-source for genuine
emergencies affecting public safety, not for operational inconvenience that
could be addressed on a reasonable timeline. An expedited RFQ with a 10-business-day
response window is feasible and would substantially reduce legal challenge risk
compared to an unchallenged sole-source determination.

### Ground (c) — No Alternative Source

The three vendor inquiry responses (MC-801619 through MC-801629) do not
adequately support ground (c). Two of the three vendors did not respond
to Facilities' inquiry email, and one responded to say it did not service
the Bradenton area. These responses do not demonstrate market-wide unavailability;
they demonstrate that Facilities contacted a limited vendor universe. Automated
Logic Corporation was not contacted. Ground (c) is not established on this record.

### Summary Assessment

The sole-source justification has a viable basis under ground (a) only if
the Automated Logic Corporation alternative is evaluated and found to be
technically inadequate or not price-competitive. Ground (b) supports urgency
but not a full sole-source determination. Ground (c) is not supported.

## Recommendation

[WORK PRODUCT] — Counsel recommends the County Administrator not approve
SS-2024-0019 as presented. Before a sole-source determination is made,
Facilities Management should be directed to:

1. Obtain a written technical scope and price from Automated Logic Corporation
   within 10 business days.

2. If Automated Logic's offering is inadequate or significantly more costly
   than IBC Corp.'s $387,500 quote, resubmit SS-2024-0019 with the Automated
   Logic evaluation as supporting documentation.

3. Alternatively, issue an expedited competitive RFQ with a 10-business-day
   response period, inviting IBC Corp., Automated Logic, and any other vendors
   who responded to the initial inquiry.

If Facilities demonstrates that the Automated Logic option is technically
infeasible, the sole-source justification under ground (a) becomes supportable
and counsel will advise on the appropriate procurement authorization form.
