## Background

Commissioner Birchwood requested an advisory opinion on February 28, 2024,
regarding Manatee County's legal authority to enact a short-term rental (STR)
registration ordinance that would require residential property owners who rent
their properties for periods of less than 30 days to register with the County,
pay a registration fee, and comply with minimum operational standards including
noise, parking, and occupancy limits.

Planning and Development Services staff has been working informally on a draft
ordinance since January 2024. The draft was submitted to this office on March 15,
2024, as a component of this advisory request. Staff has identified four legal
questions that require resolution before the draft can be presented to the Board
for first reading. This opinion addresses all four.

## Legal Analysis

### Legal Question 1 — Authority Under Fla. Stat. §509.032

Florida Statute §509.032(7)(b) preempts local governments from regulating
the rates, standards, or requirements of short-term rentals that were otherwise
permissible under local law on June 1, 2011. However, §509.032(7)(b)(2) was
amended in 2021 (CS/SB 522, codified at §509.032(7)(b)(2)) to restore limited
local authority to adopt registration requirements for short-term rentals,
including the ability to impose registration fees, provided the requirements
do not address rates, standards of cleanliness, or building standards separately
regulated by the Florida Building Code.

Counsel's analysis: The proposed ordinance falls within the 2021 restored
authority because it addresses registration and operational standards
(noise, parking, occupancy) rather than rental rates, cleanliness standards,
or building standards. The ordinance draft correctly limits its scope to
these permissible areas. Legal Question 1 is resolved favorably.

However, counsel recommends adding a savings clause to the ordinance: "Nothing
in this Ordinance shall be construed to regulate the rates, cleanliness standards,
or building standards of short-term rental units, which remain solely within
the jurisdiction of the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation
pursuant to Fla. Stat. §509.032." This savings clause reduces preemption
challenge risk.

### Legal Question 2 — Equal Protection and Takings Concerns

The draft ordinance applies only to residential properties rented for periods
of less than 30 days. Planning staff asks whether the 30-day threshold creates
an equal protection problem by treating short-term rental operators differently
from long-term rental operators.

Under the rational-basis equal protection standard applicable to economic
regulations, a 30-day threshold is constitutionally defensible because it
tracks the standard Florida Tourism and Development tax threshold (Fla. Stat.
§125.0104) and is consistent with the hotel and transient occupancy regulatory
framework. The legislative history of §509.032 uses a 30-day threshold as
the definitional boundary between transient and non-transient lodging. Legal
Question 2 is resolved favorably — the 30-day threshold is constitutionally
sound.

On takings: a registration requirement does not constitute a taking because
it does not deprive the property owner of all economically beneficial use.
Hillsborough County's 2019 STR ordinance was challenged on takings grounds
and survived. Collier County's 2021 STR registration requirement similarly
was not found to constitute a taking. Legal Question 2 is resolved favorably.

### Legal Question 3 — Registration Fee Setting

The draft ordinance proposes a registration fee of $250 per year per STR unit.
Planning staff asks whether this fee requires a nexus to actual County cost
and how that nexus is established.

Under Florida law, a regulatory fee must be reasonably calculated to recover
the cost of administering the regulatory program. An arbitrary fee with no
relationship to regulatory cost is an unauthorized tax. Counsel recommends
that the Board direct staff to prepare a fee study quantifying the estimated
cost of registering and inspecting STR units before the ordinance is adopted
with a specific fee amount. The fee study should account for: (a) initial
registration processing (estimated 1.5 staff hours per application); (b) annual
renewal processing (0.5 hours); (c) complaint-response inspections (estimated
1.2 inspections per unit per year at 2 hours each); and (d) database and
technology overhead. The current draft's $250 fee appears in the right range
based on comparable county programs, but this office cannot certify it without
a formal cost study. Counsel recommends the draft be presented with a fee TBD
pending the cost study, and the ordinance amended to set the specific fee by
resolution after the study is completed.

### Legal Question 4 — Enforcement Mechanisms

The draft ordinance proposes civil fines of $500 per day for operating without
registration and $1,000 per day for repeat violations. Planning staff asks
whether these fines must go through the Special Magistrate process under
Ch. 2-26 or can be collected administratively.

Under Manatee County LDC §6.4(a), civil penalties for code violations are
enforced through the Special Magistrate. The proposed STR fines are civil
penalties for code violations (operating without registration). They should
route through the Special Magistrate unless the Board adopts a separate
administrative enforcement track. An administrative enforcement track requires
a due process mechanism — notice, opportunity to be heard, and appeal rights.
The current draft does not include an administrative hearing mechanism.

Counsel recommends that the ordinance adopt the existing Special Magistrate
enforcement track under LDC §6.4 rather than create a parallel administrative
track. This minimizes legal challenge risk and leverages existing infrastructure.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel advises the Board that the proposed
STR registration ordinance is legally defensible with the following modifications:

1. Add the savings clause regarding preempted subjects (Legal Question 1).
2. Direct staff to complete a registration-cost study before setting the $250
   fee; present the ordinance to the Board with the fee to be set by resolution
   after the study (Legal Question 3).
3. Amend the enforcement provisions to expressly adopt the LDC §6.4 Special
   Magistrate track rather than create a new administrative track (Legal Question 4).

With these modifications, the ordinance is ready for a first reading at the
Board's discretion. Counsel will work with Planning staff to incorporate the
revisions into the draft before the first reading is scheduled. Contact Marcus
Holloway at (941) 555-0407 to coordinate.
