## Background

Manatee County awarded CSCA-2022-0088 to Lakeview Paving Contractors LLC
on February 28, 2022, following a competitive procurement (IFB-2022-0011).
The contract covered milling and resurfacing services for 23 arterial road
segments totaling approximately 87 lane-miles in District 4. The base contract
amount was $4,287,000, with a 10% contingency of $428,700 for authorized
change orders.

Contract work was substantially completed on September 12, 2023. Lakeview
submitted a final pay application on October 3, 2023, for $614,800 in excess
of the contract amount and the authorized contingency. The excess claim arises
from three sources, which Lakeview characterizes as compensable changed
conditions under CSCA-2022-0088 §17 (Differing Site Conditions) and §18
(Changes in the Work):

1. **Asphalt Price Escalation** ($318,500): Lakeview argues that rapid
   asphalt binder price increases between Q3 2022 and Q2 2023 constituted
   a Type II differing site condition under §17(b). The base contract was
   bid in January 2022 when binder prices were at $X; by peak in Q2 2023,
   binder prices had increased approximately 62%.

2. **Milling Depth Overrun** ($187,400): On 7 of 23 road segments, Lakeview
   encountered existing pavement layers thicker than shown on the project
   documents, requiring additional milling passes. Lakeview claims the
   additional milling constitutes a Type I differing site condition (unknown
   subsurface or latent conditions).

3. **Haul Distance Premium** ($108,900): The asphalt plant listed in
   Lakeview's bid (Synthetic Ready-Mix Plant, 3.4 miles from centroid of
   work zone) permanently closed in March 2023. Lakeview hauled asphalt from
   an alternate plant 11.8 miles away for the final 4 months of the project.
   Lakeview claims the haul distance increase is compensable as a County-caused
   disruption.

## Legal Analysis

### Claim 1 — Asphalt Price Escalation

The contract does not include a price escalation clause. CSCA-2022-0088 §5(b)
states that the unit prices are "firm, fixed, and not subject to escalation
for material cost changes." Lakewide attempts to reframe the price increase
as a Type II differing site condition, but Florida courts and standard
construction law treatises hold that commodity price volatility is a market
risk the contractor assumes when submitting a firm-fixed-price bid. A Type II
differing site condition refers to conditions at the physical site that differ
materially from what is ordinarily encountered — not market price fluctuations.

Claim 1 has no legal basis under the contract's plain terms. Counsel recommends
rejecting this claim in full ($318,500).

### Claim 2 — Milling Depth Overrun

Type I differing site conditions — actual physical conditions at the site
that differ materially from those indicated in the contract documents — are
compensable under CSCA-2022-0088 §17(a) and established Florida construction
law. The threshold question is whether the project documents adequately
indicated existing pavement depths.

The project documents (referenced in MC-903464 through MC-903472) include
a General Notes sheet that states: "Milling depths shown are estimated. Contractor
shall verify existing pavement depths by core samples and shall notify the
County immediately if actual depths differ from plans." Lakeview submitted
no core sample pre-excavation data and did not provide the contractually
required pre-notification before encountering the thicker-than-expected pavement
on 7 of 23 segments.

The engineering staff review (MC-903488 through MC-903499) confirms that
the actual milling depth overruns are genuine and measurable, ranging from
0.7 to 1.4 additional inches per pass on the 7 affected segments. The staff
reviewer estimates the compensable value at $103,200 — not Lakeview's claim
of $187,400 — because Lakeview did not follow the core-sample and notification
protocol that would have allowed the County to adjust the plan or authorize
additional work before the overrun was incurred.

Claim 2 is partially compensable. Counsel recommends accepting the County
engineer's assessed value of $103,200 rather than Lakeview's claimed $187,400,
based on Lakeview's failure to follow the contractual notification procedure.

### Claim 3 — Haul Distance Premium

The asphalt plant closure was a third-party commercial event entirely outside
the County's control. CSCA-2022-0088 §18 authorizes compensation for "Changes
in the Work" directed by the County. The County did not direct Lakeview to
use the Synthetic Ready-Mix Plant and did not cause the plant closure. Haul
distance premium resulting from a supplier's business decision is a contractor
risk under a firm-fixed-price contract. No County-caused disruption occurred.

Claim 3 has no legal basis. Counsel recommends rejecting this claim in full
($108,900).

### Settlement Posture

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — The total amount Lakeview claims is $614,800.
Counsel assesses the legally supportable recovery at $103,200 (Claim 2,
adjusted). If Lakeview escalates to a formal dispute under CSCA-2022-0088
§20 and ultimately to circuit court, the County's litigation position is strong
on Claims 1 and 3. On Claim 2, the County's position is defensible but the
engineering-value dispute ($187,400 vs. $103,200) introduces some uncertainty
because Lakeview could argue the core-sample protocol was informational, not
a condition precedent to compensation.

Litigation of the full $614,800 claim would cost the County an estimated
$60,000 to $90,000 in outside counsel and expert fees for a matter where the
County's exposure is $103,200 at most. A settlement at $115,000 (Claim 2
adjusted plus a minor settlement premium) would represent a defensible and
cost-efficient resolution. If the Board prefers to litigate to deny the
claim entirely, the litigation cost exceeds the adjusted claim value.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel recommends the Board authorize
the Purchasing Director to offer Lakeview a settlement of $103,200 to $115,000,
inclusive of all claims and releases. If Lakeview rejects the settlement offer,

under CSCA-2022-0088 §20.

Counsel is available for an executive session discussion of the settlement
