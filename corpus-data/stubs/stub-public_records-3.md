## Legal Analysis

### Issue (a) — Personal Email Account Scope

Florida's public records law applies to records made or received by an agency
in connection with official business, regardless of the medium or whether the
record is stored on County equipment. Florida Statute §119.011(12) defines
"public record" to include any material "made or received pursuant to law
or ordinance or in connection with the transaction of official business."

Florida courts have held (Rhea v. Dist. Bd. of Trustees of Santa Fe Coll.,
et al., 109 So. 3d 851 (Fla. 1st DCA 2013)) that emails sent from personal
accounts by public officials concerning official business are public records.
The County's email retention and public records policy (MC-230525 through
MC-230531) requires County employees to forward any official-business emails
sent from personal accounts to their County email account within 24 hours.

Counsel's guidance: IT Services should query the Public Works Director's
County email account on the County Exchange server for responsive records,
as that is within the County's immediate custodial control. IT Services should
separately notify the Public Works Director that, if he used any personal
email account for Fixture Drive project communications during the request
period, he is required to identify and forward those emails to IT Services
for processing and disclosure. The Director should be instructed in writing
and should acknowledge receipt of the instruction. Failure to preserve or
produce personal-account emails used for official business could expose the
County to spoliation findings in any subsequent litigation.

### Issue (b) — Attorney-Client Communications in Email Archive

The County Attorney's 2022 correspondence with Public Works re: Fixture Drive
(MC-230555 through MC-230578) includes 23 emails exchanged between the Public
Works Director and this office from March 2022 through August 2022, addressing
legal strategy regarding a contractor dispute on that project. Those communications
are attorney-client privileged under Fla. Stat. §90.502 and §119.071(1)(d)
and are not subject to disclosure.

The broader email set (estimated 1,400 to 2,200 responsive emails based on
IT's preliminary keyword search) will include additional communications with
this office that have not yet been individually reviewed. Counsel's guidance:
IT Services should apply a custodian filter — flag any email in the responsive
set where any recipient or sender address is in the @manatee-attorney.gov
domain (synthetic domain used per ADR-003) — and submit those flagged emails
to the County Attorney's office for individual privilege review before production.
Emails that are not flagged by the custodian filter may be produced after
redaction of any exempt personal information (home addresses, SSNs, medical
information).

### Issue (c) — Processing Timeline

The volume estimate of 1,400 to 2,200 responsive emails, plus attachment
review, makes same-week or even same-month full production impractical without
extraordinary resource commitment. Chapter 119 requires production in a
reasonable time. AGO 2019-07 addresses high-volume email requests and provides
that an agency should communicate a production schedule to the requestor,
offer rolling production of non-privileged records, and document its processing
efforts contemporaneously.

Counsel recommends the following timeline:

- January 31, 2024: IT Services sends requestor an acknowledgment letter
  estimating 6-8 weeks for full production due to volume, describing the
  rolling production approach, and offering to prioritize any specific date
  range or sender/recipient if the requestor narrows the request.
- February 16, 2024: First production tranche — emails from January-June 2022
  that do not require attorney-client review (approximately 600 emails estimated).
- February 28, 2024: Attorney-client review of flagged emails from IT custodian
  filter completed. Second production tranche — non-privileged emails from
  July-December 2022 plus any non-privileged flagged emails cleared for production.
- March 15, 2024: Final production — January-December 2023 emails.

Each production tranche should be accompanied by a redaction log for any
withheld or redacted records, in compliance with AGO 2016-14 standards.

### Additional Risk Factor — Litigation Hold

Counsel notes that the scope and specificity of PRR-2024-0102 — seeking all
communications related to the Fixture Drive project over a two-year period —
is consistent with pre-litigation discovery by a party who may intend to file
a claim related to the Fixture Drive contractor dispute. Counsel recommends
that IT Services immediately issue a litigation hold on all documents responsive
to this request and related project files, preserving them against deletion
under the County's standard 60-day email purge policy. The hold should remain
in effect until counsel advises that no litigation is anticipated.
