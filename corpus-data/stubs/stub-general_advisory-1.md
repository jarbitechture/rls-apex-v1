## Request Summary

Commissioner Whaley requested a written advisory opinion on March 1, 2024,
regarding the Board's legal authority to impose a temporary moratorium on
the collection of road and school impact fees for residential development
permits. Specifically, the Commissioner seeks guidance on: (1) whether the
Board has authority under Florida law to suspend impact fee collection; (2)
if such authority exists, what procedural steps are required; and (3) whether
a moratorium could create liability for the County from developers who have
already submitted applications or reserved capacity.

## Background on Impact Fees in Manatee County

Manatee County collects road impact fees pursuant to Ch. 2-26 Procedure
26-118.001 and school impact fees pursuant to an interlocal agreement with
the Manatee County School Board dated July 15, 2019 (recorded in Official
Records Book 2841, Page 4412, Manatee County Public Records). Road impact
fees are levied under the authority of Fla. Stat. §163.31801 (Florida Impact
Fee Act). The current road impact fee schedule was adopted by Resolution
R-2023-047 on September 26, 2023.

The school impact fee collection is governed by both the interlocal agreement
and Fla. Stat. §1013.60, which authorizes school boards to enter into
interlocal agreements for impact fee collection by the county.

## Legal Analysis

### Issue 1 — Authority to Impose a Moratorium

The Florida Impact Fee Act (§163.31801) restricts local governments from
increasing impact fees more than 50% within a 12-month period and prohibits
increases more than 25% without specified findings. However, the statute does
not expressly prohibit a temporary reduction or suspension of impact fees.

Florida Attorney General Opinion AGO 2022-04 addresses a similar question
in the context of a county considering a temporary impact fee suspension to
stimulate construction during an economic downturn. The AGO concluded that
counties have general home rule authority under Article VIII, §1(f) of the
Florida Constitution to adjust or suspend impact fees for a defined period,
provided the suspension is supported by a rational basis related to a legitimate
government interest and is not arbitrary or discriminatory.

Counsel's analysis: The Board has authority under Florida home rule and Ch.
163 Fla. Stat. to impose a temporary impact fee moratorium for road impact
fees, provided: (a) the moratorium has a defined duration; (b) it is supported
by a rational basis finding (e.g., stated development incentive or economic
development purpose); and (c) it is prospective and applies uniformly to
similarly situated applicants.

School impact fees present a different analysis. The school impact fee is
collected pursuant to the interlocal agreement with the School Board. Manatee
County cannot unilaterally suspend collection without the School Board's
concurrence, because the School Board is the beneficiary of those fees and
the agreement defines the collection obligation as mandatory. The Board would
need to negotiate an amendment to the interlocal agreement before a school
impact fee moratorium could take effect. The current interlocal agreement
contains a termination and amendment clause requiring 180-day notice for
material amendments (§12 of the 2019 agreement).

### Issue 2 — Procedural Requirements

To impose a road impact fee moratorium, the Board would need to:

1. Adopt a resolution finding a rational basis for the suspension (e.g.,
   stimulating attainable housing production or addressing construction cost
   inflation). The resolution should identify the duration of the moratorium
   (recommend no longer than 24 months), the category of development subject
   to the moratorium, and the fee categories affected.

2. Provide notice consistent with §163.31801 and the County's public hearing
   procedures under LDC §2.1.

3. Determine whether the moratorium applies to all residential development or
   only qualifying attainable housing units — a means-tested or income-restricted
   moratorium reduces legal risk by narrowing the beneficiary class.

4. For school impact fees, initiate the 180-day amendment process with the
   School Board and do not represent to applicants that school impact fees
   are suspended until the amendment is executed.

### Issue 3 — Developer Reliance and Vesting Claims

The moratorium presents two categories of developer reliance risk.

**Category A — Prior applications in queue.** Developers who submitted
applications before the moratorium but have not yet paid impact fees may have
a reliance claim if they relied on the existing fee schedule in their financial
pro formas. A retroactive moratorium (applying the suspension to fees not yet
paid) would benefit these applicants, not harm them — so reliance claims do
not arise in this direction.

**Category B — Applications received during the moratorium and after termination.**
When the moratorium ends, developers who began projects during the moratorium
will face the restored fee schedule. If any developer executed land purchase
contracts or financing commitments premised on continued fee suspension, the
restoration could give rise to a vested rights claim. Counsel recommends
the resolution include explicit language stating that the moratorium confers
no vested right to perpetual suspension and that all applicants are on notice
that the fee schedule will be restored at the end of the moratorium period.

**Reserved-capacity claims.** The school capacity reservation system operates
separately from impact fee collection. School Board counsel should be consulted
before any moratorium is implemented that could affect developers' expectations
regarding school capacity reservations tied to their impact fee payment timing.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel advises:

1. The Board has legal authority to impose a temporary road impact fee
   moratorium by resolution, with appropriate findings and a defined duration.
   A 12-month moratorium is legally defensible and minimizes sunset risk.

2. A school impact fee moratorium requires School Board concurrence and at
   least 180 days of amendment process. The Board should initiate discussions
   with School Board counsel before publicly committing to include school
   fees in any moratorium.

3. The moratorium resolution should include explicit language disclaiming
   vested rights and confirming that the fee schedule will be restored.

4. Commissioner Whaley is advised to discuss the initiative with other
   Commissioners before bringing it to the full Board to assess consensus
   and any additional guidance needs.
