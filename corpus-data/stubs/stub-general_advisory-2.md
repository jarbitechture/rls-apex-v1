Outside Employment Disclosure form to Human Resources, disclosing that he

## Legal Analysis

### Applicable Law — Fla. Stat. §112.313

Florida Statute §112.313(3) prohibits a public employee from acting in a
private capacity to provide services to a business entity that is subject to
the regulation of the employee's agency. §112.313(7) prohibits a public employee
from having a contractual relationship with a business entity that creates a
continuing or frequently recurring conflict with the employee's public duties.

The key question is whether Synthesis Design Studio LLC's clients — private
applicants seeking building permits — are "business entities subject to the
regulation of" the Building and Permitting Division. The Building and Permitting
Division reviews and issues permits; applicants are subject to that regulatory
function. Driftwood, as a Senior Plans Reviewer, directly participates in
that regulatory function.

FCOE Advisory Opinion CEO-2018-09 (relevant excerpt at MC-450836 through MC-450840)
addressed a similar fact pattern involving a county building inspector who
provided private code consulting to permit applicants in the same county where
she worked. The FCOE concluded that the consulting arrangement violated §112.313(3)
because the private clients were subject to the regulation of the employee's
agency, regardless of whether the employee personally reviewed any specific
client's file.

FCOE Advisory Opinion CEO-2021-14 (MC-450841 through MC-450848) distinguished
a situation in which a building official provided code consulting services
in a different jurisdiction with no regulatory relationship to the official's
employing agency, finding no §112.313(3) violation. The distinguishing factor
was the absence of a regulatory relationship between the consulting clients
and the official's employing agency.

### Application to Driftwood

Driftwood's consulting clients are specifically seeking guidance on LDC
compliance before submitting to the County's Building and Permitting Division.
The Division — Driftwood's employing agency — reviews those same applications.
Under CEO-2018-09, this arrangement squarely violates §112.313(3). The fact
that Driftwood may not personally review his consulting clients' applications
does not cure the violation. The FCOE has consistently held that §112.313(3)
applies to the agency's regulatory function, not only to the individual
employee's specific caseload.

Additionally, Procedure 26-104.012 §3(b) requires pre-approval of outside
employment. Driftwood began this outside work in March 2024 without seeking
pre-approval and disclosed it voluntarily only in June 2024. This is an
independent violation of County personnel policy.

### Potential Exposure

Fla. Stat. §112.317 authorizes the FCOE to impose civil penalties of up to
$10,000 per violation plus removal from office for violations of §112.313.
The criminal penalties under §112.313(9) for willful violations include
misdemeanor of the first degree. Counsel does not assess the violation as
willful — Driftwood's voluntary disclosure suggests he sought guidance once
he became aware of concern — but the distinction between knowing and willful
must be evaluated based on his specific knowledge and intent.

The County's exposure in a worst-case scenario is reputational (a public FCOE
finding naming the County's failure to catch or prevent the arrangement).
The County itself is not subject to criminal liability for an employee's
§112.313 violation, but it could face scrutiny if it is found to have implicitly
ratified the arrangement by failing to act after learning of it.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel recommends the County Administrator
direct the following sequence:

1. Issue Driftwood a written notice that the outside employment as disclosed
   violates Fla. Stat. §112.313(3) and Procedure 26-104.012, and that he
   must cease the consulting activity effective immediately. Allow 5 business
   days for cessation.

2. Direct HR to document that the County acted promptly upon receiving the
   voluntary disclosure and required immediate cessation. This documentation
   protects the County from a FCOE finding that it ratified the conduct.

3. Do not refer the matter to the FCOE at this stage. The voluntary disclosure
   and prompt County response support a good-faith posture. If Driftwood fails
   to cease the outside employment, escalate to a mandatory referral.

4. Amend Procedure 26-104.012 to require periodic (annual) outside employment
   disclosures for all employees in regulatory positions, rather than only
   voluntary disclosure. This reduces recurrence risk across all permit-issuing
   staff.

Counsel will prepare the written cessation notice and a corrective personnel
action memo for HR Director signature. Contact Rowan Castellanos at
(941) 555-0615 for drafting coordination.
