## Background

Code Enforcement issued NOV 2023-CE-0891 on August 1, 2023, citing Manatee
County LDC §5.2(d) and §5.3(a) for unauthorized land grading and filling
operations conducted without a Grading, Drainage and Erosion Control Permit
(GDECP) issued under Ch. 2-26 Procedure 26-112.001. The notice was personally
served on the property owner on August 3, 2023.

The physical complaint arose from grading activity conducted in late June and
July 2023, during which the owner raised the rear yard grade by approximately
18 to 24 inches using fill dirt hauled by a contractor (Benchmark Excavation
LLC, also synthetic). After the grading was complete, stormwater runoff that
previously sheet-flowed across the property began to concentrate and discharge
onto the adjacent Redbrick property, flooding a detached workshop and causing
approximately $6,200 in claimed personal property damage.

## Legal Analysis

### Jurisdictional Status

NOV 2023-CE-0891 was timely appealed. The property owner filed an administrative
review request on August 15, 2023, twelve days after personal service, which is
within the fifteen-day window prescribed by Procedure 26-104.001 §3(b). The
Special Magistrate's jurisdiction is properly invoked, and no procedural
dismissal argument is available to Code Enforcement on timeliness grounds.

### Merits of the Grading Violation

LDC §5.2(d) prohibits grading or filling operations that alter the natural
drainage of a parcel without a GDECP. The Code Enforcement file is strong: the
LiDAR comparison (MC-310062 through MC-310069) documents a mean grade change
of 21.4 inches in the rear yard, which exceeds the 12-inch threshold that
triggers GDECP requirements under LDC §5.3(a). The contractor's delivery
records (MC-310070 through MC-310076) confirm that approximately 185 cubic
yards of fill were hauled and placed without any permit on file.

The property owner's consultant (MC-310086 through MC-310094) argues that
the grade change is less than 12 inches at the property boundary and therefore
no GDECP was required. The County's own engineering assessment (MC-310077
through MC-310085) rebuts this by measuring grade at the drainage swale
centerline, which the LDC uses as the reference datum. The County's
measurement methodology is consistent with prior Special Magistrate rulings
in RLS-22-0199 and RLS-21-0307.

### Third-Party Damage Claims

The adjacent property owner's $6,200 damage claim is a civil tort matter
between private parties. It is NOT a Code Enforcement civil penalty issue.
Counsel notes, however, that the County's grading violation finding may
establish a predicate for the Redbrick civil negligence claim. Code Enforcement
should document this relationship clearly for the record without volunteering
legal advice to either party.

### Civil Penalty Range

Under LDC §6.4(b), the Special Magistrate may impose a civil penalty of up
to $1,000 per day for first-offense grading violations. The grading activity
spanned approximately 45 days (June 15 through July 31, 2023). A per-day
penalty would be grossly disproportionate; the customary approach for a
first-offense grading violation without prior consent has been a one-time
penalty of $2,500 to $5,000 plus full remediation. Code Enforcement staff
is recommending $4,000 plus a remediation compliance order.

## Settlement Posture

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Prior to the administrative appeal, the
property owner's counsel contacted this office with a proposal: the owner
would remove the excess fill, restore the pre-grading drainage pattern under
County engineer supervision, and pay a reduced civil penalty of $1,500. Counsel
evaluates this as an acceptable resolution if the remediation timeline is
enforceable and bonded. A compliance bond of $8,000 to $12,000, forfeitable
if remediation is incomplete within 90 days, would adequately protect the
County's interest and the adjacent property. This settlement posture is
privileged and must not be disclosed in any public hearing or shared with
the Redbrick claimant.

## Analogous Matters

This matter is analogous to RLS-22-0199 (resolved 2022-11), in which the
Special Magistrate upheld a grading violation under LDC §5.2(d) and ordered
remediation plus a $3,200 one-time penalty. It is distinguishable from
RLS-21-0307, where the property owner had obtained a GDECP but did not comply
with its conditions; that case involved a penalty reduction for partial
compliance. The current matter involves a completely unpermitted grading
operation and is closer to RLS-22-0199 on the facts.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel recommends accepting the property
owner's settlement offer in principle, subject to (a) a remediation compliance
bond of $10,000 forfeitable within 90 days, (b) a negotiated civil penalty
of $2,500 to reflect the first-offense nature of the violation, and (c) written
acknowledgment by the property owner that the remediation does not resolve
or bar any civil claims by adjacent property owners.

If the Special Magistrate hearing proceeds, Code Enforcement should rely
on the County LiDAR comparison and Public Works engineering assessment as
the primary evidentiary record. The contractor delivery records provide
strong corroborating evidence of the fill volume that makes the property
owner's under-12-inch argument difficult to sustain.

Counsel will draft a proposed settlement stipulation for Code Enforcement
Director review within seven business days of receiving approval to proceed.
