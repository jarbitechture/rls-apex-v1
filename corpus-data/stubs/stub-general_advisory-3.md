## Legal Analysis

### Statutory Framework — §112.3143

Florida Statute §112.3143(3) prohibits a county commissioner from voting on
a matter that would inure to the "special private gain or loss of a principal
by whom the officer is retained" or of certain relatives. §112.3143(3)(a)
specifically covers gain or loss to a principal by whom the officer is retained
or to a relative.

"Relative" is defined at §112.312(22) to include a spouse. Franklin Newbridge,
as the Commissioner's spouse, is a relative under the statute.

The operative question is whether approval of the developer's agreement would
"inure to the special private gain or loss" of Franklin Newbridge as a relative.

### The Indirect Benefit Analysis

The chain of causation is: Board approval of the developer's agreement →
Synthesis Residential Partners LLC proceeds with the development → Synthesis
Realty Services LLC obtains the leasing contract → Franklin Newbridge earns
commissions from leasing activity → Franklin Newbridge receives special private
gain.

FCOE Advisory Opinion CEO-2020-07 (MC-560381 through MC-560387) addresses
a similar multi-step causation chain. The FCOE held that a voting conflict
is triggered when the chain of causation between the official vote and the
relative's financial gain is sufficiently direct and foreseeable. The FCOE
distinguished between speculative future benefit (which does not trigger
the conflict) and reasonably foreseeable benefit that flows directly from
the approved action.

On the facts here: the developer's agreement identifies Synthesis Realty
Services LLC as the exclusive leasing agent (see MC-560365). The leasing
contract, while not yet executed, is contemplated in the developer's agreement
itself. The connection between the Board's approval and Franklin Newbridge's
future commission income is more than speculative — it is explicitly documented
in the transaction. This chain is sufficiently direct under CEO-2020-07 to
trigger §112.3143.

FCOE Advisory Opinion CEO-2016-13 (MC-560388 through MC-560398) distinguishes
cases where a relative works for a company that has a separate, independent
business relationship with a contracting party, with no disclosed connection
between the vote and the relative's specific compensation. That distinguishing
fact is not present here — the leasing arrangement naming Synthesis Realty
Services LLC (Franklin Newbridge's employer) is a disclosed term of the
developer's agreement before the Board.

### Abstention Procedure

If a voting conflict exists, §112.3143(3) requires Commissioner Newbridge
to: (a) abstain from voting on the matter; (b) disclose the conflict prior
to the vote (Form 8B — Memorandum of Voting Conflict); and (c) file Form 8B
with the Clerk within 15 days of the vote. The abstention is mandatory, not
discretionary, when a voting conflict is identified under §112.3143.

The Commissioner may participate in discussion on the item before the vote
if she discloses the conflict at the outset of the discussion. She may not
vote.

### Participation in Pre-Vote Discussion

FCOE guidance (CEO-2021-02) confirms that a commissioner with a §112.3143
conflict may participate in deliberations on the item, but must: (a) announce
the conflict before speaking on the item; (b) not seek to influence other
commissioners' votes in a manner that would indirectly benefit the conflicted
relative; and (c) file Form 8B documenting the conflict and abstention.

## Recommendation

[ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED] — Counsel's assessment is that a voting conflict
exists under §112.3143(3) and that Commissioner Newbridge must abstain from
voting on Agenda Item 7B at the November 5, 2024 Board meeting.

The Commissioner should take the following steps before the November 5 meeting:

1. Complete and submit Form 8B (Memorandum of Voting Conflict) to the Clerk
   no later than October 29, 2024, identifying the nature of the conflict
   as the potential special private gain to her spouse through his commission
   income from Synthesis Realty Services LLC.

2. At the November 5 meeting, announce the conflict at the outset of any
   discussion of Item 7B, before making any remarks on the item.

3. Leave the voting table (or remain and formally abstain) when the vote is
   called. Either approach satisfies §112.3143(3); counsel recommends formal
   abstention on the record rather than physical departure.

4. File the completed Form 8B with the Clerk within 15 days after the meeting.

Counsel will prepare a draft Form 8B for Commissioner Newbridge's review and
signature. Contact Adelaide Frost at 941-555-0224 to coordinate. The Form 8B
draft should be reviewed and returned to counsel no later than October 25, 2024.
