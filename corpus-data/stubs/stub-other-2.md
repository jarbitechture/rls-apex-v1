## Background

Manatee County and the City of Palmetto entered an Intergovernmental Agreement
on July 14, 2016, for the joint provision of fire protection and emergency
medical services in the Station 21 response district, which straddles the
municipal boundary. Under the original IGA and Amendments 1 and 2, Manatee
County staffs Station 21 with County Firefighter-Paramedics, and the City of
Palmetto reimburses the County for a prorated share of personnel and apparatus
costs calculated on a call-volume basis.

The City of Palmetto has proposed Amendment 3, which would:

1. Revise the cost-allocation formula from a call-volume basis to a
   square-mileage basis, shifting approximately $214,000 per year in costs
   from the County to the City.

2. Relocate Station 21 from its current location at 1100 Synthetic Road (County-owned)
   to a new facility at 450 Fixture Avenue (City-owned), which is 0.7 miles
   closer to the highest-density portion of the City's service area.

3. Extend the IGA term from its current expiration of December 31, 2025,
   to December 31, 2030.

4. Include a new mutual aid provision allowing either party to request
   temporary transfer of apparatus and personnel for catastrophic events,
   with cost reimbursement at the requesting party's fully loaded cost rate.

The County Administrator has requested a legal review of Amendment 3 before
the County Attorney's office approves it for Board consideration.

## Legal Analysis

### Provision 1 — Cost-Allocation Formula Revision

The shift from call-volume to square-mileage basis changes the financial
relationship materially. The County's own analysis (MC-780942 through MC-780950)
confirms that the square-mileage formula produces a $214,000 annual increase
in City cost and corresponding decrease in County net cost, based on current
service levels.

Under Fla. Stat. §163.01 (Florida Interlocal Cooperation Act), intergovernmental
agreements must be approved by the governing body of each participating local
government. Amendment 3's formula change is a material modification that
requires Board approval, not merely County Administrator signature authority.
This is noted for procedural completeness.

The legal validity of the formula change is not in question. Both parties have
agreed to the change; the question is adequacy of documentation and approval
authority. Counsel recommends the County Administrator confirm that the City
of Palmetto City Commission has also approved or will approve Amendment 3
before the County Board votes, so that both approvals occur in sequence.

### Provision 2 — Station 21 Relocation

The proposed relocation from 1100 Synthetic Road (County-owned) to 450 Fixture
Avenue (City-owned) raises two issues: response time impact and County asset
disposition.

**Response time impact.** The County Fire & EMS response time analysis
(MC-780951 through MC-780959) shows that the 0.7-mile relocation improves
mean response time for City calls by 1.4 minutes but degrades mean response
time for County unincorporated calls in the northern portion of the district
by 2.1 minutes. The National Fire Protection Association NFPA 1710 standard
sets a 4-minute travel time goal for engine company response to structure fires.
The analysis shows the northern County area currently averages 3.8 minutes;
after relocation it would average 5.9 minutes — outside the NFPA goal.

Counsel recommends the County Administrator obtain a formal Fire & EMS waiver
analysis from the County Fire Chief before agreeing to the relocation. NFPA
1710 compliance is not legally mandatory under Florida law (local governments
are not required by statute to comply with NFPA standards), but departure from
the standard creates potential liability exposure in the event of a structure
fire response in the degraded-service area. The Board should be advised of this
tradeoff before voting.

**County asset disposition.** If Station 21 moves to City-owned property, what
happens to the existing County-owned station at 1100 Synthetic Road? The draft
Amendment 3 is silent on this point. Counsel recommends the amendment include
a provision addressing the disposition or alternative use of the County station
property. Options include: (a) County retains the property for other purposes;
(b) County leases the property to the City or another public agency; (c)
County surpluses the property through a public bid process. Leaving the property
disposition unaddressed creates ambiguity if the relationship later deteriorates.

### Provision 3 — Term Extension to 2030

A five-year extension is reasonable and consistent with the 2021 Amendment 2
five-year extension. The extension should include a termination-for-convenience
clause allowing either party to exit with 180-day written notice, which is
the current IGA standard. The draft Amendment 3 retains this provision at
§11.2 of the original IGA. No legal issue identified.

### Provision 4 — Mutual Aid and Catastrophic Event Response

The proposed mutual aid provision at §4(d) of Amendment 3 provides that
apparatus and personnel may be temporarily transferred at the requesting party's
"fully loaded cost rate." Counsel recommends defining "fully loaded cost rate"
by formula in the amendment rather than by reference to future budgets. A
suggested formula: personnel costs at the transferring agency's actual budgeted
hourly rate for each classification (Firefighter-Paramedic, Fire Lieutenant,
etc.) plus apparatus costs at the County's standard fleet cost-per-hour rate
as published annually. This avoids disputes about what "fully loaded" means
in any given year.

Additionally, the mutual aid provision should include a limit on the maximum
duration of a temporary transfer (recommend 30 days per event) and clarification
that the provision does not create a permanent obligation to transfer personnel
on demand.

## Recommendation

[WORK PRODUCT] — Counsel recommends the County Administrator approve Amendment 3
for Board consideration, conditioned on the following revisions:

1. Obtain City Commission approval before scheduling the County Board vote.
2. Require Fire Chief's formal response-time waiver analysis and present the
   NFPA 1710 tradeoff to the Board before the vote.
3. Add a property disposition provision for 1100 Synthetic Road.
4. Define "fully loaded cost rate" by formula and add a 30-day transfer duration limit.

With these four modifications, Amendment 3 is legally sufficient. Counsel
will provide a redlined version of the draft incorporating revisions within
seven business days of receiving direction to proceed. Contact Priya Sunderland
at (941) 555-0519 to confirm.
