## Background

Building Permit BP-2023-14882 was issued on April 14, 2023, for the
construction of a new two-story, single-family residence at 785 Fixture Drive.
The permit was issued under the standard residential review track with no
special conditions noted. Construction began in early May 2023 and proceeded
through framing completion by late August 2023.

On October 2, 2023, a Building Department inspector conducting a framing
inspection observed that the structure's second-floor plate height exceeded
the approved plans by approximately 14 inches, bringing the ridge line to
approximately 38.7 feet above finished grade. LDC §5.9(c)(1) establishes a
maximum building height of 35 feet in the applicable Residential General (RG)
zoning district. The inspector issued Stop-Work Order SWO-2023-0448 that same
day and posted it at the site.

Precision Frame & Form LLC has challenged the stop-work order through
Procedure 26-104.001 §5(a), which provides for administrative review of
stop-work orders within ten business days of issuance. The challenge was filed
October 13, 2023, within the ten-business-day window.

## Legal Analysis

### Validity of the Stop-Work Order

LDC §6.5(a) authorizes Building Department inspectors to issue stop-work
orders when construction does not comply with approved plans or applicable
code. The October 2 inspection documented a height discrepancy that, if
confirmed, constitutes a deviation from approved plans. The stop-work order
is facially valid and was properly posted under Procedure 26-104.001 §4(b).

### The Height Discrepancy — Factual Dispute

The contractor's engineer-of-record letter (MC-512812 through MC-512820) argues
that the inspector measured plate height from the garage slab — which the
engineer asserts is not "finished grade" for LDC §5.9(c)(1) purposes — and
that when measured from the mean finished grade of the surrounding natural
terrain, the ridge line is 34.8 feet, which is within the 35-foot limit.

The Building Department's peer review (MC-512821 through MC-512831) accepts
that the definitional dispute regarding "finished grade" is genuine. LDC
§5.9(c)(1) defines "building height" as the vertical distance from "finished
grade at the base of the structure" to the highest point of the roof ridge.
The Department's peer reviewer notes that the garage slab is the structure's
lowest finished floor, which is the reference datum the Department has used
consistently for structures with below-grade garages since a 2018 policy
memorandum. That policy memorandum is Exhibit B of MC-512821.

The legal question is whether the 2018 policy memorandum is a valid
interpretation of LDC §5.9(c)(1) or an ultra vires administrative
refinement inconsistent with the plain text of the code. This office's
analysis follows.

### Statutory Construction — "Finished Grade at the Base of the Structure"

Under Florida statutory construction doctrine applicable to local land
development codes, ambiguous text is construed in favor of the property
owner. The phrase "finished grade at the base of the structure" is ambiguous
when the structure has multiple ground-level floor elevations (garage versus
living space). Both the garage slab interpretation and the mean finished grade
interpretation are reasonable readings of the plain text.

However, the 2018 policy memorandum, while not subject to formal rulemaking
under Ch. 120 Fla. Stat., represents a consistent, publicly available, and
long-applied administrative interpretation. Florida courts give substantial
deference to an agency's consistent long-standing interpretation of its own
regulatory text, particularly when that interpretation was applied at the time
of permitting. Because the permit was issued under the 2018 policy memorandum
framework, and the applicant did not challenge that framework at the time of
permit issuance, there is a colorable argument that the applicant is estopped
from challenging the measurement standard now.

### Practical Resolution Path

If the stop-work order challenge hearing proceeds before the Building Code
Board of Adjustment, the outcome turns on whether the Board follows the
2018 policy memorandum or accepts the contractor's mean-grade argument. The
record is split and the case is not clear-cut for either side.

The most expedient resolution is a survey-based determination: the County's
Chief Building Official may accept a licensed surveyor's certification of
mean finished grade at the structure perimeter. If the surveyor certifies
a mean finished grade that, when used as the reference datum, produces a
ridge height of 35 feet or less, the Building Official may administratively
release the stop-work order on a finding of substantial compliance.

## Recommendation

[WORK PRODUCT] — Counsel recommends that the Building Department offer
the contractor a 30-day window to submit a Florida-licensed surveyor
certification of finished grade using the mean finished grade methodology.
If the surveyor's certification confirms ridge height at 35 feet or under,
the stop-work order should be released with conditions requiring re-inspection
of all subsequent work phases against the certified survey datum. If the
surveyor's certification confirms the 38.7-foot ridge under either methodology,
the stop-work order should be upheld and the contractor directed to reduce the
ridge height to comply with the 35-foot limit before proceeding.

Counsel does not recommend taking this matter to the Board of Adjustment
at this stage, as the administrative resolution path is less costly and
produces an equally enforceable outcome. If the contractor refuses the
survey path, escalate to the Board of Adjustment hearing.
