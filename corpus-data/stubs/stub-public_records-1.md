## Background

On June 3, 2024, the County Clerk's office received Chapter 119 Public Records
Request PRR-2024-0341 from Lena Synthfeld. The request specifies the following
categories of records:

> "All performance evaluation forms, internal complaints, contract non-conformance
> reports, contractor corrective action requests, and correspondence related to
> any contract awarded by Manatee County to Consolidated Power Systems Corp.
> from January 1, 2020 through the date of this request."

Consolidated Power Systems Corp. (also synthetic) was awarded three contracts
during the requested period: CSCA-2020-0012 (HVAC maintenance), CSCA-2021-0078
(generator maintenance), and CSCA-2024-0037 (the recently awarded emergency
generator contract that is currently subject to a bid protest).

The Clerk's office escalated this request to the County Attorney's office on
June 5, 2024, seeking guidance on three issues: (1) whether records related
to the pending bid protest are exempt from disclosure; (2) whether contractor
performance evaluation forms authored by County employees contain attorney-client
privileged communications; and (3) whether records related to CSCA-2021-0078
can be located and produced within the statutory timeframe.

## Legal Analysis

### Issue 1 — Pending Bid Protest Exemption

Florida Statute §119.071(1)(b) exempts competitive bid solicitation records
from public disclosure until the agency's decision to award or reject all
bids is final. The exemption covers "sealed bids" and related documents
through the point of award. The bid protest under ITB-2024-0037 was filed
on March 22, 2024, and remains unresolved as of this memorandum (see related
matter MC-700314 through MC-700381).

Counsel's analysis: The §119.071(1)(b) exemption lasts until the County
"awards or rejects all bids." A pending bid protest effectively holds the
award decision open. The CSCA-2024-0037 award has not been finalized because
the protest resolution is pending before the Purchasing Director. Therefore,
the CSCA-2024-0037 bid file and protest documents (MC-011238 through MC-011249)
are currently exempt from Chapter 119 disclosure. The Clerk may notify the
requestor that these records will be made available following final award
determination.

### Issue 2 — Attorney-Client Privilege in Performance Evaluation Forms

The CSCA-2020-0012 and CSCA-2021-0078 performance files contain several
types of documents:

- Standardized contractor performance evaluation forms (Purchasing Form PF-14)
  completed by County Project Managers — not attorney communications; no
  privilege applies.
- Non-conformance reports (NCR forms) completed by Facilities staff — not
  attorney communications; no privilege applies.
- Corrective action requests (CAR forms) jointly signed by Facilities staff
  and Purchasing — not attorney communications; no privilege applies.
- One email thread in the CSCA-2021-0078 file (MC-011228 through MC-011231)
  from Purchasing Director to County Attorney's office seeking legal advice
  regarding whether a pattern of contractor NCRs justified early contract
  termination — this communication is attorney-client privileged under
  Fla. Stat. §90.502.

The bulk of the performance file records are subject to disclosure. The single
attorney-client email thread (MC-011228 through MC-011231) should be withheld.
A proper redaction log should be provided to the requestor identifying the
withheld document by category and statutory basis without disclosing the
privileged content. The redaction log entry should state: "Email thread,
4 pages, June 2022, withheld: attorney-client communication pursuant to
Fla. Stat. §90.502 and §119.071(1)(d)."

### Issue 3 — Production Timeline

The CSCA-2021-0078 performance file has not been fully centralized. Purchasing
staff have located file materials in three locations: the Purchasing Division
central file room, the Facilities Management Division project file for the
generator maintenance contract, and a SharePoint folder maintained by the
Project Manager who has since transferred to another division. Assembling
and reviewing these materials for any additional privilege issues requires
an estimated 8 to 12 business hours.

Under Chapter 119, the County must respond to a public records request "in
a reasonable time." The statutory 10-day window is a goal, not an absolute
deadline, for complex multi-file requests. Counsel recommends the Clerk notify
the requestor within the June 13 deadline that: (a) records from CSCA-2020-0012
and portions of CSCA-2021-0078 will be produced by June 21, 2024; (b) remaining
CSCA-2021-0078 records are being assembled and will be produced no later than
June 28, 2024; (c) CSCA-2024-0037 records are currently exempt pending bid
award finalization.

This phased production approach is consistent with AGO 2014-09 (multiple-agency
records coordination does not excuse delay but allows phased release when
assembly requires reasonable time).
