## Threshold Question

The substantive merits of the alleged violation are not before the Board
today. The threshold question is whether the special magistrate's jurisdiction
has been properly invoked, given the timing of the property owner's request
for administrative review.

Under Procedure 26-104.001 §3(b), an administrative review request must be
filed within fifteen (15) calendar days of the date the Notice of Violation
is served. The NOV in this matter was served by certified mail on November 9,
2023, with a delivery confirmation date of November 13, 2023. The property
owner's request for administrative review was filed on November 30, 2023 —
seventeen calendar days after service, two days outside the procedural window.

## Legal Analysis

Three lines of analysis are relevant.

### 1. Strict construction of the 15-day window

Florida appellate courts have held in analogous code-enforcement contexts that
administrative-review windows are jurisdictional in nature. The seminal
Florida authority in this area treats the deadline as a substantive limit on
the magistrate's authority to hear the matter, not a procedural defect that
can be cured by waiver or estoppel. Applied here, the 17-day filing would
foreclose the request, and the original NOV would proceed to the special
magistrate for enforcement on its merits.

### 2. Equitable tolling

The property owner's counsel has invoked equitable tolling, arguing that
service was effective on November 13 (delivery date) rather than November 9
(mail date). Under that reading, the request was filed within 17 calendar
days of service but within 15 business days. The plain text of Procedure
26-104.001 §3(b) measures the window in calendar days from "date the Notice
of Violation is served" — and Florida case law in our jurisdiction has
generally treated certified-mail service as effective on the date of mailing
when the recipient signs for delivery within the presumptive five-day window.
On that reading, equitable tolling is not available.

### 3. The Vested Rights doctrine (preserved for the merits hearing)

If the special magistrate accepts jurisdiction, the property owner's strongest
substantive argument is vested rights with respect to the 2022 carport
installation. That argument requires evidence that (a) the County made an
affirmative representation that the carport was permitted, (b) the owner
reasonably relied on that representation, and (c) the reliance was
substantial. We have file material going to (a) — specifically, an email
from a now-departed Building Department staff member dated 2022-05-03 — that
would need to be reviewed for privilege before disclosure in the merits
phase. The Bates range MC-104320 through MC-104324 contains that email and
related correspondence. The Vested Rights analysis is preserved here for
completeness but is not before the Board on the jurisdictional question.
